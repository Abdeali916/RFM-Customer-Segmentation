# Business Problem Document
## RFM Customer Segmentation & E-commerce Analytics

### 1. Business Context

The business operates in an online retail environment and generates a large volume of customer transaction data. The available transaction records contain information about invoices, products, quantities, prices, transaction dates, customers, and countries.

Although this data captures customer purchasing activity, raw transaction records do not clearly show which customers are most valuable, which customers are loyal, which customers are becoming inactive, or where opportunities exist to improve customer retention and revenue.

The business therefore needs a structured analytical approach to convert transaction data into meaningful customer and sales insights.

---

### 2. Business Problem

The central business problem is to understand customer purchasing behavior and identify customer groups with different levels of value and engagement.

Without customer segmentation, the business may apply the same marketing or retention strategy to all customers, even though customers differ significantly in purchase frequency, spending, and recency of activity.

The business needs to answer questions such as:

- Who are the most valuable customers?
- Which customers purchase frequently?
- Which customers have recently purchased?
- Which customers may be becoming inactive?
- Which customers contribute the most revenue?
- How are sales and customer activity changing over time?
- Which products and countries contribute significantly to business performance?
- How can customers be grouped into actionable segments?

### 6. RFM Methodology

The core analytical technique used in this project is **RFM analysis**.

#### Recency

Recency measures how recently a customer made a purchase.

A lower recency value generally indicates that the customer purchased more recently.

#### Frequency

Frequency measures how often a customer makes purchases.

A higher frequency indicates stronger purchasing engagement.

#### Monetary

Monetary measures the total amount of revenue generated by a customer.

A higher monetary value indicates greater customer contribution to revenue.

The three dimensions are combined to create an RFM score and classify customers into behavioral segments.

---

### 7. Customer Segmentation

RFM scores are used to group customers into actionable segments.

Potential segments include:

| Segment | Business Interpretation |
|---|---|
| High-Value Customers | Customers with strong purchasing value and engagement |
| Loyal Customers | Customers who purchase frequently and consistently |
| Potential High-Value | Customers showing characteristics of valuable customers |
| Recent Customers | Customers who have purchased recently |
| At-Risk Customers | Previously valuable/active customers showing weaker recent activity |
| Inactive Customers | Customers with low recent purchasing activity |

The exact segment definitions are determined by the RFM scoring logic implemented in the project.

### 8. Data Preparation

Before performing the analysis, the transaction data is prepared to improve analytical reliability.

The preparation process includes:

- Inspecting the dataset structure.
- Checking missing values.
- Handling missing customer identifiers.
- Identifying duplicate records.
- Reviewing cancelled transactions.
- Validating quantity and price values.
- Calculating transaction-level revenue.
- Preparing customer-level data for RFM analysis.

The cleaned data is then used for exploratory analysis, SQL analysis, RFM segmentation, and Power BI visualization.
